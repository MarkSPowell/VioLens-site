Once the page has loaded it even works offline. - q: What is a SAZ file? a: | A `.saz` file is a Fiddler Session Archive, saved by Fiddler Classic, Fiddler Everywhere or VioLens. It's a zip file with a `raw/` folder holding each session's request (`_c.txt`), response (`_s.txt`) and timings and flags (`_m.xml`). - q: What is a HAR file? a: | A `.har` (HTTP Archive) file is a JSON record of web requests and responses. Browser developer tools can export one, and support teams often ask for it. Open it here to see each request, its headers, and any bodies the file includes. Some HAR files leave bodies out. - q: How do I open a Chrome or Edge net-export log? a: | Open the `.json` or `.jsonl` file saved by `chrome://net-export` or `edge://net-export` here. The page lists the URL requests, with their headers, redirects and network errors where the log has them. It doesn't show DNS, socket or other low-level events. For those, use Chromium's [NetLog viewer](https://netlog-viewer.appspot.com/). - q: Why are bodies missing from my NetLog? a: | A NetLog only has response bodies if it was captured with **Include raw bytes** turned on, which also records cookies and sign-in details. This page doesn't show request bodies from a NetLog. Requests answered from the browser's cache may have no status or headers. - q: Can I clean a NetLog or convert it to HAR here? a: | No. Sensitive data can appear in many places in a NetLog, so treat a log captured with cookies or raw bytes as private. - q: How do I remove cookies from a HAR file? a: | Open the `.har` file here, tick **Remove cookies and sign-in headers from downloads**, and click **Download cleaned HAR**. You get a new file, and your original isn't changed. Only the sessions that match the filter are included. Read the next answer before you share it. - q: How does the check for leftover secrets work? a: | Open a SAZ or HAR file and click **Check for leftover secrets**. It searches what you would download as HAR, using the current filter and cleaning choice. A SAZ made here from a HAR holds no more of the capture than that. The check doesn't cover an original SAZ file as a whole: WebSocket messages and CONNECT sessions, for example, aren't in the HAR download. It looks for cookie and sign-in headers, some recognizable kinds of tokens and keys, and values with names such as `password` or `access_token`. Results show where each one is without showing the whole value. It runs in your browser and doesn't change the file. It can miss secrets, doesn't look for personal data in general, and can't search binary bodies, so finding nothing doesn't mean the file is safe. - q: How do I convert SAZ to HAR? a: | Open the `.saz` file here, type in the filter box if you only want some of the sessions, and click **Download HAR**. Bodies are decompressed, text is saved as text and anything else as base64, and timings and comments are kept. CONNECT tunnels are left out, as they are in browsers' HAR files. - q: Is it safe to send the HAR file to someone? a: | Check it first. A capture can hold passwords, session cookies and personal data. Tick **Remove cookies and sign-in headers from downloads** before you download to blank the `Cookie`, `Set-Cookie`, `Authorization` and common API key and token headers. It doesn't change URLs, bodies, comments, WebSocket messages, other headers or other fields in the file, so filter out sessions you don't need, run **Check for leftover secrets** and look through the rest. - q: Why won't my file open? a: | HAR files must be valid JSON. HAR and NetLog files can be no bigger than 256 MB. A NetLog that was cut short can often still be partly read. For SAZ files, the viewer can't open password-protected SAZ files, so save it again from Fiddler without a password. Files that add up to more than 1 GB when unzipped are too big for a browser tab. If your browser can't decompress a body (not every browser supports Brotli or zstd yet), that body is shown as it was saved. - q: How do I convert HAR to SAZ? a: | Open the `.har` file here and click **Download SAZ**. Only sessions that match the filter are included, and the cleaning checkbox applies too. Request and response headers, status codes, comments, timings and the bodies in the HAR are carried over. HAR bodies are already decompressed, so the headers describing compression and length are updated to match. SAZ can't hold everything in a HAR, and bodies the HAR left out stay missing. This converter also leaves out WebSocket messages, page details and extra fields, so keep your original HAR. ---
